i recently got accepted in google summer of code and upon posting this news, i got lots of people asking me, "what is gsoc and how do we get in?"

before i tell you how to get into gsoc, let me tell you what it really is and what it actually means to get in it (i'll keep things short).

GSoC, short for Google Summer of Code, is a program organized by Google to introduce new developers to <a href="#open-source"><u>open source</u></a>.

Google gathers organizations with different <a href="#open-source"><u>open source</u></a> projects that allow people like you and me to participate in making <a href="#contributions"><u>contributions</u></a>.

these organizations submit their projects to Google that they would like contributors to work on.

contributors make <a href="#proposal"><u>proposal</u></a>s to work on the projects accepted by Google and the accepted contributors' work is sponsored by Google in the form of <a href="#stipend"><u>stipend</u></a>s depending upon the <a href="#size"><u>size</u></a> of each project.

this year, around 15,245 applicants from 131 countries submitted a total of 23,371 <a href="#proposal"><u>proposal</u></a>s which were reviewed by over 2,000 mentors and org admins from 184 organizations. 

these are some **BIG** numbers.

and only 1,141 contributors were selected -- **an acceptance rate of 7.48%**!

so when you ask "how do i get into gsoc?", what you are really asking is "how do i be among that top 4%?"

this year, the Open Healthcare Network Foundation, the organization i got accepted into, only had one project and around 8,700+ contributors in the slack community. 

of course not all the 8.7k+ people applied for gsoc but this is just to give you an idea of the size of each organization.

# now that you have a rough idea of what we are talking about, let me tell you, what i think, got me accepted into gsoc:

1. i was genuinely curious and
2. i did my own research: read old threads, went through docs, read code and only then
3. i asked the right questions (that weren't asked before.)
4. i helped new members out by properly welcoming them and providing them the resources to help them get started and aswered their queries.
5. i helped establish some discipline by reminding unaware contributors about the rules and the guidelines.
6. i was focusing on having fun and i prayed for it

# which led to:

- other contributors also started welcoming new members and started answering queries 
- new contributors felt welcomed and encouraged to make <a href="#contributions"><u>contributions</u></a>.
- i was approached by several people asking for guidance 
- some people even thought i was part of the core team (one guy asked me "why no outside contributors were selected this year?" implying that i am employed by ohcn... like what?!)
- someone from the core team even thanked me for doing good work as a community manager :D
- unaware contributors started following guidelines and some even helped enforce them.

# my advice:

1. be an awesome person who helps everyone and brings a positive energy
2. learn and strictly follow, or at least try to follow, the contribution rules and guidelines of your organization.
3. do your research: go through old threads, open up repos and go through the code, read old issues and prs, closed and open both.
4. save the core team from headaches by answering general queries or enforcing guidelines etc.
5. try and have fun
6. don't forget that you are a part of a community, so be kind to your fellow contributors :D
7. don't completely rely on ai
8. keep all conversations public and avoid dm'ing anyone unless absolutely personal and necessary!
9. you can even email previous year's selected contributors for the same organization and ask for tips (i did it and it really helped!)

and it's not the end of the world if you didn't get selected this year, i really hope that you do next year, but please don't get discouraged. keep contributing, focus on having fun and i am sure you will get in :)

and remember, it's a collabortation not a competition ;)

thank you!

# glossary:

1. <u id="open-source" style={{scrollMarginTop: '96px'}}>open source</u>: software with all its code public for anyone to modify, extend and use.
2. <u id="contributions" style={{scrollMarginTop: '96px'}}>contributions</u>: writing code, updating docs, reporting bugs or helping people out in the community etc
3. <u id="proposal" style={{scrollMarginTop: '96px'}}>proposal</u>: a document submitted by each contributor against a project, outlining everything from project details, implementation plan to timeline and milestones. each contributor can submit upto 3 proposals in the same or different organization, but only 1 proposal per project.
4. <u id="stipend" style={{scrollMarginTop: '96px'}}>stipend</u>: an amount paid by Google divided into two parts according to the <a href="#size"><u>size</u></a> of the project, ranging from 750$ to 3,300$. the exact amount also depends on your country's buying power.
5. <u id="size" style={{scrollMarginTop: '96px'}}>size</u>: projects are divided into 3 sizes according to hours: small (~90 hours), medium (~175 hours), and large (~350 hours).
